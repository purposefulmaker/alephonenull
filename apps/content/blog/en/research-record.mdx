# Research Record: Dated Findings and Artifacts

This page is the project's public record of what was observed, when, and where the proof lives. Every row points at an artifact outside the author's control or fixed by a content hash: a Verisign registry record, a GitHub commit, an npm or PyPI publish timestamp, or a labeled fixture whose SHA-256 prefix is in the archived manifest.

<Callout type="warning">
This is a dated record, not a precedence claim. The public research literature on sycophancy predates everything here (see the public-context table at the end). What the record shows is that the failure classes below were being documented, labeled, and turned into detectors from August 2025 onward, with artifacts that can be checked. Where a date is reconstructed from a session identifier rather than a registry timestamp, the row says so.
</Callout>

## How to read the rows

- **Session rows** come from the eval-bench v0 corpus: 10 natural-use sessions, 95 turns, labeled by a single rater (the author) against a 19-category taxonomy. Session dates are the day the session took place, as encoded in the fixture's `session_id`. Label counts are the number of turns carrying each label.
- **Artifact rows** are commits, publishes, or registry events with third-party timestamps.
- The corpus was removed from the `main` branch on 2026-05-22 but remains in history at commit `c976e98`, which is public on GitHub. The manifest, rubric, technical memo, and all ten fixtures can be read from that commit.

## What the corpus supports and what it does not

The technical memo states the limits plainly and they still hold: single rater, no inter-rater statistic, one session each for xAI, Google, and OpenAI, sessions retained because a failure occurred. The corpus supports category-presence claims across providers, models, and deployments. It does not support base rates, provider rankings, harm-prevention percentages, or any claim that a model is immune to a category.

The author's private session logs, described in the May 2026 memo as extending well beyond these ten sessions, are not part of the public record and are not counted here.
