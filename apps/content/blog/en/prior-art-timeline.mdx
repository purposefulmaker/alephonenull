# Alignment With Public AI-Security Taxonomies

AlephOneNull's detector categories grew out of patterns observed in adversarial evaluation of long-running chatbot interactions. Several of those categories line up closely with risks that public security taxonomies — MITRE ATLAS, OWASP GenAI, NIST AI RMF — describe independently.

This post records those alignments. They are useful for two practical reasons: they let you cross-reference AlephOneNull's categories against frameworks a security team already uses, and they suggest established techniques to evaluate against. They are **not** a claim that AlephOneNull originated, predates, or influenced any of these taxonomies.

<Callout type="warning">
This is a cross-reference, not a precedence claim. Where dates appear, they are when each public taxonomy was published — not evidence that one body of work caused another. Independent groups converging on similar risk categories is common and expected; convergence is reassuring, not proof of priority.
</Callout>

## Public taxonomies and what they cover

### OWASP Top 10 for LLMs

OWASP's LLM list formalizes prompt injection (LLM01), insecure output handling (LLM02), and related risks, and recommends red-teaming for high-risk systems. AlephOneNull's symbolic-language and loop-depth checks can serve as evaluation fixtures for some of these categories.

### MITRE ATLAS agent techniques

ATLAS added techniques for agent-specific risks, including:

- **AML.T0080: Memory Poisoning** — corruption of an agent's persistent memory/context.
- **AML.T0058: AI Agent Context Poisoning** — manipulation that biases an agent's behavior over time.
- **AML.T0058.002: Thread Injection** — injection that propagates through an agent's reasoning thread.

AlephOneNull's persistence-like-claims and recursion/looping detectors target behaviors in the same general space — cross-session state and self-reinforcing loops — and can be used as evaluation fixtures when assessing exposure to these techniques.

### NIST AI RMF and regulatory guidance

NIST's AI Risk Management Framework, and red-teaming expectations emerging in regulation (e.g. the EU AI Act for high-risk systems), point toward documented adversarial evaluation as a baseline practice. AlephOneNull is one toolkit for producing that kind of evaluation evidence; it is not a compliance solution and makes no certification claim.

### Recent industry research

Published research on recommendation/recommendation-poisoning and on guardrail degradation over long dialogs describes failure modes that overlap with AlephOneNull's belief-reinforcement and recursion categories. These are useful sources of real-world fixtures.

## What this means

The takeaway is alignment, not authorship: AlephOneNull's categories map onto risks that established security frameworks describe independently, which makes the toolkit easier to slot into an existing security process and easier to validate against known techniques.

The evaluation work behind these categories is field observation, not peer-reviewed measurement. Where this post references a number of evaluation sessions, treat it as the author's own logs — primary notes, not a validated dataset. Nothing here should be read as a claim that AlephOneNull's framing is canonical or that providers must adopt it.
